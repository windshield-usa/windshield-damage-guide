Windshield Damage Guide for Drivers

Windshield damage can begin as a small chip and become a larger crack because of road vibration, temperature changes, moisture, or additional impact. Understanding the damage can help drivers decide when to request a professional inspection.

When Windshield Repair May Be Considered

Repair may sometimes be possible when the damage is limited, has not spread significantly, and does not interfere with the driver's primary viewing area. The final decision should be made after the glass is inspected by a qualified auto-glass technician.

When Replacement May Be Necessary

Replacement may be recommended when the crack is spreading, the glass has multiple damaged areas, the inner glass layer is affected, or the damage could interfere with safe visibility and windshield performance.

Important: Do not rely only on photographs or online advice. Windshield damage should be professionally evaluated before a repair or replacement decision is made.

Why ADAS Calibration Matters

Many modern vehicles have cameras or sensors positioned near the windshield. Depending on the vehicle, these systems may support lane assistance, forward-collision alerts, traffic-sign recognition, or automatic emergency braking.

When windshield replacement changes the position of a camera or sensor, calibration may be required according to the vehicle's procedures.

What Drivers Should Do After Windshield Damage

  1. Photograph the damaged area.
  2. Avoid pressing or touching the chip or crack.
  3. Keep moisture and dirt away from the damaged area.
  4. Check whether glass coverage is included in your insurance policy.
  5. Arrange a professional auto-glass inspection.
